Forward Deployed Engineering

AI implementation is field work.

The job is not “add an agent.” The job is to find operational leverage, decide where AI belongs, prove it, and land it inside the team.

Tighter version of the deck. Less role-definition repetition; more operating playbook.
Thesis

The model is not the moat. Deployment is.

01
Audit

Find where work actually happens: volume, delays, exceptions, hidden judgment, and money-at-risk.

02
Decide

Classify every step as human, deterministic software, AI endpoint, or a controlled hybrid.

03
Prove

Build evals, guardrails, traces, and staged rollout before asking the business to trust it.

Where to look

Start with volume, not vibes.

Find the teams drowning in repeat work

  • High ticket / email / case volume
  • Repeated copying between systems
  • Backlogs, SLA misses, manual QA queues
  • Expensive specialists doing clerical work
  • Lots of rework from inconsistent inputs
  • Decision latency that blocks revenue or operations

Do not begin with

  • “Where can we use agents?”
  • Executive anecdotes only
  • One-off workflows with no owner
  • Processes where success cannot be measured
The FDE starts like an operator: where is volume, pain, and measurable economic drag?
Project selection

Score opportunities before writing code.

A good FDE wedge is high-volume, measurable, owned, and messy enough that the current process leaks value.

5
Volume: weekly cases, users, dollars touched
4
Pain: backlog, rework, cycle time, escalation
4
Measurable: clear pass/fail and baseline
3
Feasible: data access, APIs, permissions
3
Owner: champion + Day 2 operator

Rule of thumb: if volume is low or ownership is absent, do not spend FDE calories there yet.

Use this to prioritize projects/teams/processes.
Embed

Sit with the team until the real workflow appears.

Observe

  • Watch 5–10 real cases end to end.
  • Ask them to narrate every click, copy, lookup, wait, and handoff.
  • Capture exceptions as first-class steps, not footnotes.
  • Compare what people say to system logs and artifacts.

Extract what lives in their head

  • “When do you pause and ask someone?”
  • “What inputs are usually wrong?”
  • “Which field would be expensive if wrong?”
  • “What do you check that is not in the SOP?”
This incorporates the user’s point: users often cannot articulate the edge cases until you watch real work.

Workflow decomposition

The solution is usually all three.

Human + software + AI is the default enterprise pattern.

The FDE’s job is to assign the right responsibility to each layer.

Human
Owns judgment, accountability, approvals, negotiations, and costly irreversible decisions.
Traditional software
Owns deterministic rules, schemas, validation, workflow state, queues, permissions, and audit logs.
AI endpoint / agent
Owns language understanding, extraction, summarization, recommendation, variable routing, and tool-assisted reasoning.
This is the major reframing: do not decide “AI or not.” Decide the mixed architecture.

High-stakes rule

If accuracy moves money, route through a human.

AI
Propose
Extract field, summarize evidence, recommend value, cite source span.
Software
Constrain
Validate schema, range, totals, duplicate risk, permissions, and audit trail.
Human
Approve
Review high-risk fields, exceptions, low-confidence outputs, and irreversible actions.

Example: AI extracts a payment amount; software checks invoice math and PO match; human approves if amount is high, confidence is low, or the vendor is new.

This directly covers the user’s “critical steps where money relies on accuracy” point.
Eval design

Evals are built from real failures, not generic prompts.

Build the golden set

  • 20–50 real cases to start; expand from production failures.
  • Include common cases, high-volume cases, and ugly exceptions.
  • Label expected output, allowed uncertainty, and human-review triggers.
  • Keep negative cases: behavior that must not happen.

Measure the right level

  • Field correctness: did it extract the right value?
  • Decision correctness: did it route/approve/escalate correctly?
  • Trace quality: did it use the right evidence and tools?
  • Ops quality: latency, cost, retries, review burden.
This aligns with agent eval best practice: curated datasets, failures, regression suite, online production traces.
Deployment

Autonomy is earned in stages.

0
Observe
Human works; FDE measures baseline.
1
Assist
AI drafts; human edits and owns action.
2
Shadow
AI runs silently on real cases; compare outcomes.
3
Approve
AI proposes; human approves bounded actions.
4
Autonomous
AI acts only inside monitored limits.

Many valuable deployments stay at assist, shadow, or approve. That is not failure; that is responsible design.

Pulled from the video theme: integrate first, shadow mode, gradual autonomy.

FDE law

“Happy path” demos are cheap.

There may be one way a workflow goes right. There are a thousand ways it goes wrong.

Find exceptions

Malformed inputs, missing records, unclear ownership, conflicting policies, stale data.

Design fallback

Ask for clarification, route to queue, block action, escalate, or degrade to deterministic mode.

Turn into evals

Every meaningful production failure should become a regression case.

This was one of the strongest video themes; make it memorable.
Artifacts

The lean FDE paper trail.

  • Opportunity scorecard: volume, pain, metric, feasibility, owner
  • Workflow trace: steps, systems, handoffs, exceptions
  • Responsibility matrix: human / software / AI / hybrid
  • Risk map: fields, actions, money-at-risk, review thresholds
  • Eval pack: golden cases, graders, pass/fail, regression suite
  • Rollout plan: observe → assist → shadow → approve → autonomy
  • Operating plan: owners, alerts, rollback, Day 2 runbook
  • Business case: cost saved, risk reduced, revenue unlocked
These are the artifacts you would actually bring to a client/team.
Close

The FDE is responsible for the boundary.

Find the highest-leverage workflow. Embed until you understand the hidden edge cases. Then assign every step to the right owner: human, software, AI, or hybrid.

Great FDE work does not maximize AI. It minimizes fragility while increasing business throughput.
