# Discover

Discover establishes a grounded view of the customer before the team proposes a solution.

## Objective

Understand the customer’s context, workflows, stakeholders, constraints, and signals of pain well enough to discuss a specific problem rather than a collection of symptoms.

## Entry conditions

- A customer opportunity, request, or observed operating need exists.
- The team has access to people, workflow evidence, and relevant operating context.
- No problem statement has yet been treated as validated.

## Questions to answer

- Who experiences the workflow, who owns its outcomes, and who can make decisions?
- What happens today, including workarounds, handoffs, systems, and constraints?
- Where does the workflow create pain, delay, risk, cost, or missed opportunity?
- What evidence distinguishes a recurring problem from an isolated complaint?

## Recommended activities

- Interview users, operators, sponsors, and technical partners in the context of their work.
- Observe the current workflow and collect concrete examples, artifacts, and baseline signals.
- Map stakeholders, decisions, systems, dependencies, and constraints.
- Separate facts, assumptions, and open questions; test the highest-risk assumptions early.

## Expected deliverables

- A concise customer and stakeholder map.
- A current-state workflow description with evidence-backed pain points.
- A discovery evidence log that records sources, observations, assumptions, and open questions.
- A candidate problem statement to refine with the customer.

## Exit criteria

- The team can name a specific, evidence-backed problem and the people affected by it.
- Stakeholders recognize the current-state description as credible.
- The most important uncertainties are explicit enough to frame the problem.

## Common failure modes

- Treating a sponsor’s requested solution as the problem without observing the workflow.
- Interviewing only decision-makers and missing the people who operate the work.
- Collecting anecdotes without recording evidence, constraints, or contradictory signals.
- Moving forward with a broad symptom list instead of a problem that can be discussed and tested.

## Related capabilities

- Discovery interviewing
- Workflow observation and mapping
- Stakeholder mapping
- Evidence synthesis

## Practice

Choose a familiar manual workflow. Draft five interview questions that reveal its users, handoffs, constraints, and pain signals, then write one evidence-backed candidate problem statement.
