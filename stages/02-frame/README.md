# Frame

Frame converts discovery evidence into an agreement about the problem worth solving and how progress will be judged.

## Objective

Turn evidence into a shared problem definition, desired outcome, success measures, scope, and decision criteria.

## Entry conditions

- Discovery has produced credible evidence about a specific customer problem.
- The affected stakeholders and operating context are understood well enough to discuss trade-offs.
- The team can distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions that still need validation.

## Questions to answer

- What problem are we solving, for whom, and why does it matter now?
- What outcome would demonstrate meaningful improvement?
- Which measures, constraints, and decision criteria will define success?
- What is in scope, explicitly out of scope, and dependent on another decision or team?

## Recommended activities

- Synthesize discovery evidence into a plain-language problem definition.
- Facilitate a working session to align sponsors, users, and delivery partners on outcomes and trade-offs.
- Define baseline measures, target signals, and how evidence will be collected.
- Record scope boundaries, assumptions, risks, and decision owners.

## Expected deliverables

- An agreed problem frame that names users, pain, context, and intended outcome.
- A success-measure definition with baselines or a plan to establish them.
- A scoped engagement statement with constraints, assumptions, dependencies, and decision criteria.
- A decision record capturing stakeholder alignment and unresolved questions.

## Exit criteria

- The customer and delivery team agree on the problem worth solving.
- Success measures and scope boundaries are concrete enough to evaluate options.
- Decision-makers know how they will judge progress and which uncertainties remain.

## Common failure modes

- Rephrasing a solution request instead of defining the underlying problem.
- Using success measures that cannot be observed, influenced, or agreed upon.
- Leaving scope implicit and discovering incompatible expectations during delivery.
- Claiming alignment because no one objected rather than obtaining an explicit decision.

## Related capabilities

- Problem framing
- Outcome and metric definition
- Workshop facilitation
- Stakeholder alignment and decision logging

## Practice

Take the candidate problem statement from Discover. Rewrite it to name the affected users, current pain, desired outcome, one measurable success signal, and one explicit out-of-scope boundary.
