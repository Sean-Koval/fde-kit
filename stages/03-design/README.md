# Design

Design makes the agreed problem frame actionable through a feasible, testable path to value.

## Objective

Explore and validate a feasible solution approach, including experience, technical boundaries, risks, and a path to value.

## Entry conditions

- The customer and delivery team agree on the problem, desired outcome, success measures, scope, and decision criteria.
- The highest-value uncertainties and constraints are visible.
- Relevant users and technical partners can evaluate candidate approaches.

## Questions to answer

- What approach can produce the desired outcome within the agreed constraints?
- How will users experience the change in their workflow?
- Which technical boundaries, integrations, data, security, or operational constraints shape the solution?
- What must be tested first to reduce delivery, adoption, or feasibility risk?

## Recommended activities

- Generate and compare a small set of solution approaches against the agreed decision criteria.
- Sketch workflow changes, service boundaries, data flows, and operational responsibilities at the fidelity needed to test them.
- Prototype or otherwise test the riskiest assumptions with real users or operations.
- Define an incremental path to value, acceptance signals, and the risks that require mitigation before build.

## Expected deliverables

- A validated solution approach tied to the agreed problem frame and success measures.
- Experience and technical boundary sketches that make the proposed change reviewable.
- A risk and assumption register with validation results and mitigation actions.
- An incremental build plan with acceptance signals for the first valuable slice.

## Exit criteria

- The approach is credible enough to implement and test with real users or operations.
- Stakeholders understand the expected workflow change, technical boundaries, and trade-offs.
- The first increment has a clear value hypothesis, acceptance signals, and feasible path to build.

## Common failure modes

- Designing a complete future state before validating the riskiest assumption.
- Producing technical diagrams that do not explain the user or operating change.
- Choosing an approach without comparing it to agreed success measures and constraints.
- Treating a prototype as proof when it has not been evaluated by relevant users or operations.

## Related capabilities

- Solution shaping
- Rapid prototyping and user validation
- Architecture and integration design
- Risk analysis and incremental planning

## Practice

For a framed problem, list two plausible solution approaches. Compare them against one success measure, one technical constraint, and one adoption risk; then identify the smallest validation experiment for the recommended approach.
