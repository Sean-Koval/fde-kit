# Executive readout

## Purpose

Communicate to sponsors and executives in one page, decision first. State what decision is requested, the recommendation, the outcome against the targets declared before the work started, what the evidence does not prove, the risks and their controls, cost and value, the next 30 days, and the asks. The readout carries the same fact base as the [business case](business-case.md) and the [evaluation pack](evaluation-pack.md); it never introduces a number that those records cannot support.

## Timing

Use at the end of [Frame](../stages/02-frame/README.md) to confirm the outcome and decision rule, at each gate decision in [Deploy](../stages/05-deploy/README.md), at the outcome review in [Enable](../stages/06-enable/README.md), and at the expand, hold, or stop decision in [Expand](../stages/07-expand/README.md). Use the weekly-status variant between those points.

## Instructions

1. Write the decision requested first. If no decision is needed, say "no decision; status only" so the audience knows how to read the page.
2. Pre-wire the decision. Before the meeting, walk each decision-maker and each stakeholder who can block through the recommendation and the evidence, one at a time, and record their objections. The meeting confirms a decision the audience has already examined; it is not where they first see the number.
3. Keep one fact base across audiences. The sponsor readout, the operations review, and the technical review use the same measures, denominators, and sources as the business case and the evaluation pack. Change the altitude, never the numbers.
4. Never present a number without its denominator, baseline, and source. "Correction rate 6.9%" is incomplete; "99 of 1,431 assisted invoices needed correction (6.9%), against 211 of 1,200 sampled invoices (17.6%) that needed rework at baseline, from the enterprise resource planning (ERP) and mailbox sample" can be checked. In the fictional LumenPeak engagement, Elena Torres approved expansion on that form of evidence, not on the headline.
5. Report every predeclared target, including the ones that were missed, and state what the evidence does not prove: cohort size, period, exclusions, seasonality, and the difference between measured results and projections.
6. Pair each risk with the control in place and its owner. A risk without a control is an ask, not a caveat.
7. Set the next 30 days with named owners. Starting heuristic, not an industry standard: 30 days is long enough to show movement and short enough that the audience will remember the commitment; adjust to the customer's decision cadence.
8. End with the asks. Each ask names the person, what they are asked to do, and by when. Keep the page to one page; details go in the linked records. The [executive communication](../skills/executive-communication.md) guide covers altitude, saying no, and handling capability expectations.

## Expected output

A one-page readout that a sponsor can act on without a meeting, with every number traceable to its source, and a three-line weekly status that keeps the same fact base between decision points.

## Template

```markdown
# Executive readout — [workflow] — [date]

- **Audience:** [names and roles]
- **Author and fact-base owner:** [name and role]
- **Pre-wired with:** [names; date; objections recorded]

## Decision requested

[Decision in one line: invest / continue / promote to the next gate / expand / hold / stop; or "no decision; status only"]
[Decision owner: name and role; decide by: YYYY-MM-DD]

## Recommendation

[One sentence: what to do and the single strongest reason]

## Outcome against predeclared targets

| Measure | Baseline and source | Target | Result | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [quality, time, adoption, control, reliability, cost] | [value; numerator/denominator; source ID; period] | [predeclared target; date agreed] | [value; numerator/denominator; period] | [met / missed / inconclusive and why] |

## What the evidence does not prove

- [cohort, period, document types, regions, or peaks not covered]
- [measured result versus projection; what the confidence interval reflects and what it does not]
- [what the system did not do: approvals, writes, autonomy not reached]

## Risks and the controls in place

| Risk | Control in place | Evidence | Owner | Residual risk accepted by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [policy, safety, adoption, reliability, cost] | [control] | [test, trace, or rehearsal] | [name] | [name and date] |

## Cost and value

- **Delivery to date and remaining:** [value; source]
- **Operating cost per unit:** [value; denominator; period]
- **Value basis:** [capacity, cost, revenue, or risk; measured or projected; range]
- **Business case:** [link and version]

## Next 30 days

| Action | Owner | Due | Evidence it will produce |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [action] | [name] | [YYYY-MM-DD] | [measure or record] |

## Asks of the audience

| Ask | Of whom | By when | If not granted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [decision, access, budget, people, or removal of a blocker] | [name] | [YYYY-MM-DD] | [what holds or slips] |

---

# Weekly status — [workflow] — [week ending YYYY-MM-DD]

1. **On track:** [yes / at risk / no — against which gate or target; numerator/denominator; source]
2. **What changed:** [one result, decision, or blocker since last week, with its evidence]
3. **Decision or help needed:** [ask, of whom, by when; or "none"]
```

## Completion checks

- [ ] The decision requested and its owner appear first; if none, the page says "no decision; status only".
- [ ] Every number has a denominator, a baseline, and a source, and matches the business case and evaluation pack.
- [ ] Missed targets and what the evidence does not prove are stated, not omitted.
- [ ] Each risk has a control, evidence, and owner; each next-30-day action and each ask has a named person and date.
- [ ] Decision-makers and potential blockers were pre-wired before the meeting and their objections recorded.
